# Using Hope

**Help an Ally:** Spend a Hope to Help an Ally who is making an action roll you could feasibly support. Describe how you're helping and roll a d6 advantage die. Any number of PCs can assist as long as they spend a Hope to do so. *If the ally has gained advantage on a roll from multiple sources, they take the highest of all the advantage dice rolled and add the result to their action roll.*  

**Use an Experience:** Use a relevant Experience on an action or reaction roll and add its modifier to the result. You can spend an additional hope to use additional relevant experiences.  

**Initiate a Tag Team Roll:** Spend *3 Hope* to initiate a Tag Team Roll between you and another PC to combine your efforts together in an exciting and scene-defining moment (see pg.97)  

**Activate a Hope Feature:** A Hope Feature is any effect that allows (or requires) you to spend Hope to activate its effects. You must spend the specified number of Hope, or you can't trigger the hope feature. When using a Hope Feature, if you rolled with Hope for that action, the Hope you gain from that roll can be spent on that feature.  

# Combat

## Attack Rolls

- Declare which weapon you'll use, and find out what character trait it relies on. Roll for that trait, adding any relevant Experiences or modifiers. The Difficulty is the target's Evasion.  
- If successful, make a damage roll (using weapon stats) to determing how much damage the target takes.  
- By default, attack rolls can only target one adversary. Some spells or abilities allow their effects to target multiple adversaries; roll once and apply that result to all of the adversaries the attack can hit.  

## Spellcast Rolls
- Used to create significant magical effects, usually with a domain card.
- Your subclass gives you a Spellcast trait -- use that trait when you make a Spellcast roll.
- Some Spellcast rolls have a set difficulty (eg. "Spellcast Roll (14)); if a Spellcast roll can damage or negatively affect a creature, it is also considered an attack roll and, unless specified, must exceed their Difficulty.

## Damage Rolls
- Your Proficiency with a weapon determines how many damage dice you roll on a successful attack with a weapon, although some abilities and spells use Proficiency as well. This value starts at 1 and is not weapon-specific; it doesn't change or reset when you change weapons.

## Damage Dice

- Any time a move tells you to deal damage using your Proficiency, you roll a number of dice equal to your Proficiency.
- Any time a move tells you to deal damage using your Spellcast trait, you roll a number of dice equal to your Spellcast trait.

**Remember: DAMAGE IS THE RESULT OF YOUR ROLL + MODIFIERS, NOT THE AMOUNT OF HIT POINTS A TARGET TAKES.**


### Critical Successes
- If your attack roll critically succeeds, you deal extra damage. Start with the highest possible value the damage dice can roll, and then make a damage roll as usual, adding it to that value.  

## Direct Damage
- Physical or magical damage that Armor Slots can't be used to reduce. For example, if a character is *Poisoned* by a creature's attack, they might take 1d10 direct physical damage each time they act, which their Armor slots can't be used to reduce.


## Evasion

Represents a character's ability to avoid attacks and other unwanted effects from adversaries. This value sets the Difficulty for any roll a creature makes against you. **You get to describe what your character's Evasion represents, flavoring it the same way you describe their actions.** 

## Hit Points and Damage Thresholds

**Hit Points** represent physical injuries and discomforts your character experiences during their adventures. When you take damage, you mark between 1 and 3 HP, representing the harm your character suffers.  

The number of HP you mark is determined by your damage thresholds.  

When I tell you to take damage, compare the damage total to your thresholds and mark a number of Hit Points determined by the thresholds:

- **Severe Damage** is equal to or above your severe threshold; mark 3 HP  
- **Major Damage** is equal to or above your Major threshold but below Severe; mark 2 HP  
- **Minor Damage** is below your Major threshold; mark 1 HP.

When you mark your last Hit Point, you must make a Death Move (pg. 106)

When you make a downtime move (pg. 105), you have the opportunity to clear some of your marked Hit Points. 

***Optional Massive Damage Rule*** can be implemented if agreed on by the table; if you ever take damage equal to double your Severe threshold, you mark 4 HP.

## Stress

**Stress** represents the mental and physical strain your character suffers during their adventures. Some class or heritage features, such as spells or abilities, require you to mark a Stress to use them. **The GM may also give you the opportunity to mark a stress to succeed at the action you're taking.** 

- When you mark a stress, describe the mental or physical burden the character is knowingly accepting; e.g. has repeatedly failing at something they're usually good at made them doubt themselves? Have they put their body in harm's way to defend an ally? Have they seen something horrifying or grotesque that will take them a long time to forget -- if they ever do? Have they decided to do something that crosses a personal moral boundary in order to contribute to a greater good or save an innocent life?  

When you mark your last stress, you become *Vulnerable* (see pg. 102, "Conditions") until you clear at least 1 stress.  

If you're ever forced to mark 1 or more Stress but your slots are already full, you must instead mark 1 Hit Point. 
  - For example, if an adversary forces you to mark 3 stress but your stress is already filled, you mark 1 Hit Point. If you would take 2 stress from an enemy and you have 1 stress slot left, you take 1 stress and 1 Hit Point.
