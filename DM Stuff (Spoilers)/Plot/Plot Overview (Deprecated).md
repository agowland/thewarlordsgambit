# Badgerbrook Besieged

The party, a group of characters forced to flee their homes and old lives due to a longing for adventure, the need to outrun debts (or more specifically, their collectors), famine, warfare, oppression, civil unrest or the law, are travelling on [[The Restless]], a combination passenger and cargo ship that travels frequently between [[The Circle]] and [[The Islands of Rockchester]]. The ship is large, able to berth fifteen passengers and twenty ship’s company. Unless there is a backstory-driven reason otherwise, the party members have purchased low-class tickets and will sleep in hammocks on their journey. The more affluent passengers have either shared or private cabins toward the middle of the ship where things are more stable. The ship is, at least in the beginning, in excellent condition, with a dependable and dedicated crew.

For running the initial passage between Faulmoth and [[Badgerbrook]], see [[1. At Sea (Prologue)]].

[[The Restless]] slips into the port at Badgerbrook late, well after the sun has dipped below the horizon and the cool wind has started to blow in from the sea. The city stretches out before you in every direction — even the docks behind you are bustling and full of life as longshoremen hustle to get the ships loaded for tomorrow’s departures. Included with the cost of your passage from Faulmoth is a night at the closest Inn, though it’s certainly not the *best*. Dragging whatever belongings you’ve brought with you, you all — party and other passengers — make your way up the hill and around a corner. Then, abruptly, Archie stops and gestures at the building behind him. “[[The Rustin’ Anchor]],” he says, by way of explanation.

**The Hook**
The party’s ship is hailed by a small skiff as they approach the harbor. A desperate page from the besieged port pleads for help, explaining that the defenders are on their last legs. The attackers, led by a ruthless warlord, have cut off supply lines and are trying to starve the town into submission. People have already started to die from hunger, and there is not much time left. Because they fly under a flag separate from The Circle or Rockchester, the party is the only hope for the people of Rockchester. *Their* ship won’t be harassed by The Circle’s navy, so long as they aren’t caught helping the besieged Rockchesterites.

**Act 1: The Beachhead**
The players must find a way to get to shore. They could be dropped off on a secluded beach away from the main conflict and sneak into town, or they could try to fight their way through the enemy ships. Once inside, they find a city in chaos, with citizens huddled in shelters, people and animals starving and laying half-dead in the streets, and the local guard scattered and demoralized. The town’s mayor, drunk and exuberantly defeatist, says that the only way to stop the siege is to secure the lighthouse and turn off the light, causing The Circle’s ships to run aground. To hear her say it, such a feat is completely impossible, so Badgerbrook, and all of Rockchester, might as well give up. Which, of course, they refuse to do, because of their pride.

**Act 2: The Counterattack**
The party learns that there were, until recently, strong locals defending Badgerbrook, though those locals are now nowhere to be found. This starts a countdown that increases with each new clue the party gets, or each secret they uncover. Each time the countdown goes up, the party member with the lowest **STAT** hears, with greater certainty, an irresistible song coming from Damp Den – a song they cannot ignore and must increasingly beg the rest of the party to follow with them. Once there, a **STANDING ROCK PUZZLE** must be solved, which leads to their meeting **AN ADVERSARY** – a powerful being who has struck a deal with The Circle to lead defenders of Rockchester astray, to distract and dazzle and confuse them, and scatter them across the lands, to anywhere but Badgerbrook. If the party can collect those defenders, *and* provide **THE ADVERSARY** with a better prize than The Circle has offered, they will be in a position to prepare to make a viable attack on the forces laying siege to Badgerbrook.

**Act 3: The Warlord’s Gambit**
The warlord retreats to a fortified position in Canter, with their forces spread throughout the city. The party learns that The Circle wants more than just plunder and land – it seeks a powerful artifact hidden somewhere in Rockchester that will make them unstoppable. The party must either find this artifact, carefully hidden from outsiders by generations of Rockchesters, and destroy it, or confront the warlord in a final epic battle to save the island, and the world, from total subjugation.

##### Influences on The Town & Map
“The town should still appear functional and complete from the outside, with stores and landmarks existing, albeit possibly damaged. Characters related to these points of interest will likewise exist” [How To Design a Town \| Beginner's Guide for Aspiring DMs](https://2minutetabletop.com/how-to-design-a-town/)

“Which buildings were most heavily targeted? How are the people reacting, and how do they react to the party?”

“The most complicated small town is the campaign introduction. This is where you should give the most planning, to put your best foot forward. Be sure to include every resource and store players might need in their first few levels, either in town or nearby. An alchemist or magical store can also be added, to open the possibility for bartering for an item or seeing something that motivates them to return later and purchase.”

“Using a small town as the campaign’s beginning requires putting effort into the characters. You want your key figures to be distinct, as they will most likely be a part of the story you are telling. They will be motivating the party in their first adventure and helping to catalyze the player characters as a group. Who you choose to fulfill these roles will depend on the introductory story. Consider the situation your party is starting in, what they will be doing in the town, and who would be most likely to deal with adventurers.”

“While cities are expansive and rarely feel the influence of a few adventurers, small towns can be drastically changed. Keep track of your players’ actions and interactions in the places they visit. They only need to include the major points that would cause a difference for characters or the location, with as much detail as you need so that you can read back later and remember it.”

“One of the most satisfying feelings as a player is being recognized as having made a difference. It’s a small gesture, but a rewarding one. This can inversely act as a way to show a real consequence for player actions, with townsfolk remembering when a party member slighted them. An example of this is when my party trespassed while investigating a kidnapping. They had acted on very little evidence and made incorrect assumptions. As a result, the owner of the property and the local lord are well aware of who the party is and do not consider them friends.”
