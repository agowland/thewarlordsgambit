#### Opening Hook
Open with an immediate, active scene that engages the players right away. Avoid starting in a tavern or with a long monologue. Instead, drop them right into the action, whether it's a tense negotiation, a puzzle, or an imminent threat.

> [!quote] 
> **At the table:** 
> *Urgent option:* To get this started, let’s roll for initiative!
> *Suspenseful option:* *Character*, why don’t you give me a perception roll?

#### Rising Action
This is the bulk of the session. Present the players with a series of escalating challenges, clues, or conflicts related to the opening hook. This is where you can spend your **Fear** to complicate the narrative and give the players opportunities to earn **Hope**.

#### Turning Point
Somewhere around the midpoint of the session, introduce a major twist. This could be a sudden betrayal, a key piece of information, or the arrival of a new, unexpected threat. This changes the players' objective and raises the stakes.

> **At the table:** Describe the turning point.


#### Climax
This is the moment where the main conflict of the session is resolved, whether through combat, a difficult social encounter, or a complex puzzle.

#### Falling Action
The immediate aftermath of the climax. Show the consequences of the players' actions. This is a moment for them to breathe, collect their thoughts, and interact with the world after the big event.


#### Cliffhanger
End the session with a tantalizing lead or a direct threat that sets the stage for the next game. Leave them with a question they want answered, a new location they need to visit, or a problem they must immediately deal with next time. If the characters are settling into a rest, foreshadow danger that might come in the night.
