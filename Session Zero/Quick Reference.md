# Quick Reference

--- 


## CHARACTER TRAITS

- **Agility:** leap, sprint, maneuver
- **Strength:** lift, smash, grapple
- **Finesse:** control, hide, tinker
- **Instinct:** perceive, sense, navigate
- **Presence:** charm, perform, deceive, intimidate
- **Knowledge:** recall, analyze, comprehend

## CLASSES & SUBCLASSES

- **Bard**: Can be a **Troubadour** (bolster allies with music) or a **Wordsmith** (captivate crowds with clever wordplay).
- **Druid**: Can be a **Warden of the Elements** (embody natural elements) or a **Warden of Renewal** (use magic to heal).
- **Guardian**: Can be a **Stalwart** (take heavy blows and keep fighting) or a **Vengeance** (strike down enemies who harm allies).
- **Ranger**: Can be a **Beastbound** (form a bond with an animal ally) or a **Wayfinder** (hunt prey and strike with deadly force).
- **Rogue**: Can be a **Nightwalker** (manipulate shadows) or a **Syndicate** (have a web of contacts).
- **Seraph**: Can be a **Winged Sentinel** (take flight for crushing blows) or a **Divine Wielder** (dominate the battlefield with a legendary weapon).
- **Sorcerer**: Can be an **Elemental Origin** (channel raw elemental power) or a **Primal Origin** (extend the versatility of spells).
- **Warrior**: Can be a **Call of the Brave** (use enemy might to fuel power) or a **Call of the Slayer** (strike down adversaries with immense force).
- **Wizard**: Can be a **School of Knowledge** (have a keen understanding of the world) or a **School of War** (utilize trained magic for violence).

## ANCESTRIES

- **Aetheris**: Humanoids with a celestial heritage and glowing auras.
    - Can clear Stress by spending Hope on a successful roll.
    - Can fly and gain an advantage on rolls to command or intimidate.
- **Clank**: Sentient mechanical beings.
    - Gain a permanent +1 bonus to a starting experience.
    - Can take a long rest move during a short rest.
- **Drakona**: Wingless, stout humanoids with elemental breath.
    - Can mark a Stress to take less severe damage.
    - Can make a ranged attack with elemental breath.
- **Dwarf**: Broad, sturdy humanoids with a mastery of stone and steel.
    - Can spend 2 Hope to gain a bonus to damage thresholds after taking damage.
    - Can strike the ground to damage and restrain multiple targets.
- **Elf**: Tall, slender humanoids with pointed ears.
    - Can take an additional downtime move during any rest.
    - Can take an additional action roll during a rest after a successful roll.
- **Faerie**: Playful, winged humanoids with vivid or shifting colors.
    - Can spend 3 Hope to reroll the Duality Dice.
    - Can mark a Stress to gain a bonus to Evasion against an attack.
- **Faun**: Humanoids with goat-like features.
    - Can call woodland creatures to aid in repelling invaders.
    - Can kick off a target to push them back and deal damage.
- **Firbolg**: Bovine humanoids with broad noses and long, drooping ears.
    - Can deal damage to all targets in an area after a successful movement roll.
    - Can communicate telepathically with nearby creatures.
- **Fungril**: Humanoids resembling mushrooms, born of rot and renewal.
    - Can communicate telepathically with other Fungril.
    - Can ignore disadvantage when making Agility rolls.
- **Galapa**: Turtle-like humanoids with large shells.
    - Gain a bonus to damage thresholds equal to their Proficiency.
    - Can retract into their shell to gain resistance to physical damage.
- **Giant**: Towering humanoids of immense strength.
    - Gain an additional Hit Point slot at character creation.
    - Can treat melee-range features as very close range.
- **Goblin**: Small, cunning humanoids with large ears.
    - Can ignore disadvantage on Agility rolls.
    - Can force an adversary to reroll an attack against them or an ally.
- **Halfling**: Small, sturdy humanoids with a warm spirit.
    - The entire party gains a Hope at the start of each session.
    - Can reroll a Hope Die that results in a 1.
- **Human**: The most common and adaptable humanoids.
    - Gain an additional Stress slot at character creation.
    - Can reroll a failed roll on an Experience.
- **Inferis**: Humanoids marked by an infernal heritage.
    - Can change a roll with Fear into a roll with Hope by marking 2 Stress.
    - Can mark a Stress to make an enemy temporarily Vulnerable after an attack.
- **Katari**: Agile, cat-like humanoids.
    - Can make an Agility roll to deal damage and make an enemy Vulnerable.
    - Can move through a target's space to deal damage.
- **Orc**: Sturdy humanoids with tusks.
    - Gain a bonus to a damage roll equal to their Proficiency.
    - Attacks against them have disadvantage when they have 1 Hit Point remaining.
- **Ribbet**: Energetic, frog-like humanoids with great agility.
    - Can leap a great distance and have advantage on Agility rolls.
    - Can give an ally a Hope after successfully helping them on a roll.
- **Simiah**: Anthropomorphic simian humanoids.
    - Can ignore disadvantage on any roll that relies on their physical traits.
    - Can use their Prehensile Tail to manipulate objects from a distance.

## COMMUNITIES

- **Duneborne**: From a community that makes their home in the desert.
    - ***Oasis:*** During a short rest, you or an ally can reroll a die used for a downtime action.
- **Freeborne**: From a collective that lived under tyrannical rule and is now liberated.
    - ***Unbound:*** Once per session, when you make an action roll with Fear, you can change it to a roll with Hope instead.
- **Frostborne**: From a place of snow and ice.
    - ***Hardy:*** Once per rest, you can Help and Ally traverse difficult terrain without spending a Hope.
- **Hearthborne**: From a small village or the countryside.
    - ***Close-knit:*** Once per long rest, you can spend any number of Hope to give an ally the same number of Hope.
- **Reborne:** You were once a member of another community, but you can no longer remember it.
	- ***Found Family:*** Once per session, you can spend a Hope to use an ally’s community ability. When you do, your ally gains a Hope.
	- At any point, when you’ve discovered the community you were once a part of, or have joined a new community, you can permanently trade this community  card for that one instead.
- **Highborne**: From a life of elegance and prestige in the upper echelons of society.
    - ***Privilege:*** You have advantage on rolls to consort with nobles, negotiate prices, or leverage your reputation to get what you want.
- **Loreborne**: From a society that favors academic or political prowess.
    - ***Well-Read:*** You have advantage on rolls that involve the history, culture, or politics of a prominent person or place.
- **Orderborne**: From a collective that focuses on discipline or faith.
    - ***Dedicated:*** Record three sayings or values your upbringing instilled in you. Once per rest, when you describe how you’re embodying one of these principles through your current action, you can roll a d20 as your Hope Die.
- **Ridgeborne**: From a community that calls the rocky peaks and cliffs of the mountainside home.
    - ***Steady:*** You have advantage on rolls to traverse dangerous cliffs and ledges, navigate harsh environments, and use your survival knowledge
- **Seaborne:** Being part of a seaborne community means you lived on or near a large body of water. 
	- ***Know the Tide:*** You can sense the ebb and flow of life. When you roll with Fear, place a token on your community card. You can hold a number of tokens equal to your level. Before you make an action roll, you can spend any number of these tokens to gain a +1 bonus to the roll for each token spent. At the end of each session, clear all unspent tokens.
- **Slyborne**: From a group that operates outside the law.
    - ***Scoundrel:*** You have advantage on rolls to negotiate with criminals, detect lies, or find a safe place to hide.
- **Underborne**: From a subterranean society.
    - ***Low-Light Living:*** When you’re in an area with low light or heavy shadow, you have advantage on rolls to hide, investigate, or perceive details within that area.
- **Wanderborne**: From a nomadic group that forgoes a permanent home.
    - ***Nomadic Pack:*** Add a Nomadic Pack to your inventory. Once per session, you can spend a Hope to reach into this pack and pull out a mundane item that’s useful to your situation. Work with the GM to figure out what item you take out.
- **Warborne**: From a place that is, or was, ravaged by war.
    - ***Brave Face:*** Once per session, when an attack would cause you to mark a Stress, you can spend a Hope instead.
- **Wildborne**: From a community that lives deep within the forest.
    - ***Lightfoot:*** Your movement is naturally silent. You have advantage on rolls to move without being heard.
	
## EXPERIENCES

- 2 allowed at character creation, each one adds a +2 modifier to a relevant character trait.

## EVASION

- How hard the character is to hit. Class default is written on each class’s character sheet.

## HIT POINTS & STRESS

- **Hit Points (HP)** represent physical harm. When you take damage, you mark off Hit Points equal to the amount of harm dealt.
- **Harm** is the term for physical damage you take. If the Hit Points you receive (after optionally using any available armour slots) are above the threshold marked on your character sheet, you mark a harm.
- **Armor** provides a Damage Threshold, which is a number that reduces the amount of harm you take.
- **Stress** represents emotional, mental, and spiritual fatigue. All characters start with a base number of Stress slots, which can be affected by your ancestry or other abilities.
- You can choose to mark Stress in order to activate certain abilities or reroll a failed die.
- You can clear Stress and Hit Points during a rest or with specific features and abilities.

## TURNS & ROLLS

### What you can do on a turn

Daggerheart does not use a rigid turn-based system. Instead, the game uses a narrative-driven "spotlight" system. There is no set limit to the number of actions you can take. You can:

- **Take a single action that requires a roll** and then your turn in the spotlight is over.
  
- **Move** up to a certain range. This can be done as a "free" action within close range or may require a roll if moving farther or in a dangerous situation.
  
- **Perform any number of abilities or actions that do not require a roll** before or after you make a roll.

### Types of Rolls

The two main types of rolls in Daggerheart are Action Rolls and Reaction Rolls. Both use the Duality Dice (one d12 for Hope and one for Fear).

- **Action Rolls**: _Used when a character takes an action with an uncertain outcome._ You roll the Duality Dice, add your relevant trait modifier, and try to beat the Difficulty set by the Game Master. The result is determined by the total number and which die is higher:
  
    - **Critical Success:** Both dice match and meet or exceed the Difficulty. You get what you wanted and a little extra. You gain a Hope and clear a Stress.
      
    - **Success with Hope:** Hope>Fear, total meets or exceeds Difficulty. You get what you wanted and you gain a Hope.
      
    - **Success with Fear:** Fear>Hope, total meets or exceeds Difficulty. You get what you wanted, but there's a consequence. The GM gains a Fear.
      
    - **Failure with Hope:** Hope>Fear, total does not meet Difficulty. You don't get what you wanted, but you still gain a Hope.
      
    - **Failure with Fear:** Fear>Hope, total does not meet Difficulty. You don't get what you wanted, and things go very badly. The GM gains a Fear.
      
- **Reaction Rolls**: _Used to respond to an attack or a hazard._ These are typically a trait roll to meet a specific Difficulty. Reaction Rolls **do not** generate Hope or Fear, and a critical success doesn't clear a Stress or gain a Hope, but it does ignore any negative effects on a success.

## COMBAT & SPOTLIGHT

- **No Initiative**: Daggerheart does not use a traditional turn-based initiative system. Instead, players act when it's narratively appropriate, and the GM can decide when to make an adversary act.
  
- **The Spotlight**: The "spotlight" is on one player at a time. The GM will ask "What do you do?" and that player can describe their actions. Once they make a roll, the spotlight can move to another player or back to the GM.
  
- **GM Moves**: The GM can make a "move" to introduce a complication or have an adversary act, especially when a player rolls with Fear or fails a roll. This keeps the game moving and the tension high.
    
    - **Show how the world reacts**: Describe an immediate change in the environment or situation based on the player's actions.
      
    - **Ask a question and build on the answer**: Engage the player in the narrative by asking a question about their character and incorporating their response.
      
    - **Put them in a tough spot**: Introduce a new obstacle, choice, or difficult situation for the player to handle.
      
    - **Reveal a secret**: The GM can share a piece of information, a hidden detail, or a truth about the world.
      
    - **Introduce a new threat**: A new enemy or danger appears on the scene.
      
    - **Use an adversary's Fear feature**: An enemy can use a special ability that is powered by the GM's Fear.
      
    - **Spotlight an adversary**: An enemy gets a turn to act in the narrative.
      
    - **Offer a dangerous bargain**: Present the player with a choice that has both an immediate benefit and a severe, negative consequence.
      
- **Teamwork**: Since there is no rigid turn order, players can easily collaborate on their actions, responding to events as they happen in a more fluid, cinematic way. The game encourages "Tag Team Rolls" where one player leads an action and others can make Reaction Rolls to help out.

## Difficulty

- **5 (Very Easy):** An action with almost no chance of failure for a trained professional. (e.g., Kicking down a flimsy, rotting door.)
  
- **10 (Easy):** A routine task with minimal risk. (e.g., Convincing a friendly guard to let you pass.)
  
- **15 (Average):** A typical challenge for an adventurer. This is the most common Difficulty. (e.g., Picking a difficult lock, or striking a monster in combat.)
  
- **20 (Hard):** A significant challenge, pushing a character to their limits. (e.g., Leaping across a wide chasm, or tricking a highly perceptive foe.)
  
- **25 (Very Hard):** A monumental task that is borderline heroic. (e.g., Navigating a complex celestial puzzle, or grappling a giant.)
  
- **30 (Nearly Impossible):** A feat that is almost unheard of. (e.g., Single-handedly lifting a massive stone gate, or convincing a king to give up his throne.)
